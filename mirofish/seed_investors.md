# New Investors Circle: Member Profiles

The New Investors Circle is a free six-week workshop at a public library in Brooklyn, New York, for adults who are just starting to invest for the long term. The organizer asked this season's members to share a short profile so the group could get to know one another. Five members agreed to be featured.

## Maya Lin

Maya Lin is a 29-year-old QA engineer at a mid-sized software company. She is single and rents an apartment with a roommate. Her salary is steady, and she is paying off a car loan and student loans, which together take about a quarter of her monthly income. Nobody depends on her financially. She has about three months of expenses saved and wants to invest $15,000 for retirement, money she does not expect to touch for about 20 years.

Maya describes herself as middle-of-the-road about risk: she accepts some ups and downs in exchange for growth but does not chase big swings. When her retirement account fell in 2022, she left it alone, though she admits she checked it more often than she liked. She runs on weekends and is slowly learning how retirement accounts work.

## Jordan Reyes

Jordan Reyes is a 24-year-old marketing coordinator who lives alone. He earns a steady salary, pays student loans that take about a fifth of his income, and has about three months of expenses saved. No one depends on him. He wants to invest $8,000 to grow his wealth over the next 30 years.

Jordan watches finance videos online and talks confidently about growth stocks, saying that market dips are buying opportunities. He mentions less often that in 2022 he sold a cryptocurrency position after it dropped sharply, and that he still regrets it. He is talkative, uses investing slang freely, and is a big basketball fan.

## Luis Ortega

Luis Ortega is a 34-year-old full-time rideshare and delivery driver. He is married with two young children who depend on his income, which changes from week to week. Car payments and credit card payments take close to half of what he earns, and he has about two weeks of expenses in savings. He has $3,000 he wants to invest to grow over the next 15 years, because he feels he has waited too long to start.

Luis has never invested before. He is eager for strong growth and believes he could ride out a market drop, although he has never been tested by one. He is not familiar with terms like "volatility" and prefers plain explanations. He enjoys cars and is always looking for new side income.

## Eleanor Park

Eleanor Park is a 52-year-old senior accountant who plans to retire at around 65. She is married, her two children are grown, and her house is paid off, so she has no debt payments and no dependents. Her income is very stable, and she keeps about nine months of expenses in cash. She plans to invest $250,000 for retirement.

Eleanor dislikes risk and would rather earn less than watch her balance swing. During the 2008 financial crisis she moved her investments to cash near the bottom of the market, a decision she partly regrets. She is precise, keeps her answers short, and is private about money, preferring words like "comfortable" to exact figures. She gardens and plans to travel in retirement.

## Tyler Brooks

Tyler Brooks is a 23-year-old master's student who works as a teaching assistant on a fixed stipend. He shares an apartment, has student loan payments that take about 30% of his income, and has about one month of expenses saved. No one depends on him. He has $5,000 that he hopes to use for a house down payment a few years after he graduates, about four years from now.

Tyler is unsure how he feels about investment risk and often answers questions with "I guess" or "it depends." He knows little about investing; his roommate told him he should buy an ETF, but he is not sure what one is. He plays video games and follows new music.
